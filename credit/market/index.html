How big is the market?

There's no single agreed number. Research firms define "debt consolidation" differently: some count only the fees and revenue providers earn, others count the value of the debt being consolidated. That's why published 2025 estimates run from about $1.3 billion to over $200 billion.

The underlying demand is large and growing. U.S. personal loan balances, the main vehicle for consolidating credit card debt, reached a record $281 billion in Q2 2026, up 9.6% year over year, according to TransUnion. The Federal Reserve put the average 24-month personal loan APR at 11.86% in May 2026, versus 22.15% on credit cards charged interest, and that spread is what drives most consolidation.

The debt consolidation workflow

Every provider packages it differently, but a consumer consolidation follows the same path from scattered balances to a single payment.

  1. Take inventory of debts

    List every balance, APR, minimum payment and due date. This sets the payoff amount and the rate the new product needs to beat.

  2. Pre-qualify with a soft credit pull

    Check rates with several lenders without affecting the credit score. Lenders estimate eligibility from credit score, income and debt-to-income ratio.

  3. Choose a consolidation path

    Compare total cost, including origination fees and term length, not just the monthly payment.

    Personal consolidation loan0% balance transfer cardHome equity loan or HELOCDebt management plan

  4. Apply and underwrite

    A hard credit pull plus income and identity verification. The lender sets the final APR, amount and term.

    If declined: the borrower typically moves to a nonprofit debt management plan through credit counseling, or, as a last resort, debt settlement or bankruptcy.

  5. Fund and pay off creditors

    Funds go directly to the old creditors (many lenders offer direct pay) or to the borrower, who then pays each balance in full.

  6. Make one fixed monthly payment

    One due date, one fixed rate, one payoff date. Autopay discounts often lower the APR further.

  7. Pay off and rebuild credit

    Lower card utilization usually lifts the credit score early on. The plan works only if the paid-off cards aren't run back up.
